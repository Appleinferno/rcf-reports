What Is Right of Way Clearing?

A right of way is the strip of land a utility company, pipeline operator, road department, or property owner has legal access to for running power lines, gas or water lines, fence lines, or shared driveways. Keeping that corridor free of trees, brush, and fast-growing vegetation is what right of way clearing is all about.

Left unmanaged, an overgrown right of way can cause power outages during storms, block emergency and utility access, trap moisture against pipelines, and create liability issues for landowners and municipalities alike. Regular clearing keeps corridors accessible, inspectable, and safe.

Instead of cutting and hauling, our crews rely on forestry mulching to grind brush and undergrowth into fine mulch right where it falls, clearing wide corridors in a single low-impact pass.

Our Right of Way Clearing Process

Every right of way project follows the same careful sequence, whether we're clearing a quarter-mile fence line or a multi-mile utility corridor for a contractor.

1. Site Walkthrough & Utility Marking

We walk the corridor with you or your contractor, confirm easement boundaries, and coordinate utility locates before any equipment moves in.

2. Selective Mulching & Vegetation Removal

Our mulching heads grind brush, saplings, and low canopy to grade, clearing the full width of the easement while leaving desirable boundary trees intact.

3. Access Corridor & Edge Clearing

We open drivable access along the corridor edge so crews and inspection vehicles can reach the line without cutting a new path through the property.

4. Debris Mulching & Site Cleanup

All material is mulched on site rather than piled or hauled, so there's no burn pile, no debris hauling fee, and no bare, eroded ground left behind.

5. Final Inspection & Maintenance Scheduling

We walk the finished corridor with you and, if requested, set up a recurring maintenance schedule to keep regrowth under control season after season.

Benefits of Professional Right of Way Clearing

A properly maintained right of way protects the infrastructure it surrounds and the people who depend on it.

  • Reduced Outage Risk — clear corridors mean fewer trees falling on lines during storms.
  • Compliance Support — vegetation kept within required clearance distances for utility and pipeline standards.
  • Improved access for inspection, repair, and emergency crews at any time of year.
  • Soil & Erosion Protection — mulch stays on site, holding moisture and preventing washouts.
  • Lower Long-Term Costs — routine mulching is far cheaper than reactive storm cleanup.
  • Wildlife & Habitat Friendly — selective clearing leaves root systems intact and supports natural regrowth.

Right of Way Clearing FAQs

How wide should a right of way be cleared?

Width depends on the easement agreement or utility standard governing the corridor — typically anywhere from 15 to 100+ feet. We confirm the required width with you before work begins.

Will forestry mulching damage my property or leave ruts?

Our forestry mulching equipment runs on low ground-pressure tracks and processes material in a single pass, so there's minimal soil disturbance compared to traditional dozer clearing.

How often should a right of way be maintained?

Most corridors need a maintenance pass every one to three years depending on vegetation type and growth rate. We're happy to set up a recurring schedule after the initial clearing.

Do you work with utility companies and contractors directly?

Yes — we regularly partner with utility crews, pipeline contractors, and municipalities. Contact us to discuss your project scope and timeline.
